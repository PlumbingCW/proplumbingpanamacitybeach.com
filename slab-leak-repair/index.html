Think You Have a Slab Leak? Act Now

Call (855) 575-2890 to talk through the problem and ask about timing. Shut off the main water valve if water is visible on the floor or the meter is spinning fast. A slab leak can run for days with no water on top, but the loss and the undermining of soil beneath the slab add up.

Stay off wet flooring, keep outlets and electrical devices out of the wet area and use the main shutoff only until the leak is found. Taking photos and noting the meter reading helps with an insurance claim.

Signs of a Leak Under the Slab

  • A warm or hot patch on the floor that does not match the sun
  • The sound of running water when every fixture is off
  • A water meter that moves with the house shut down
  • Cracked tile, lifted flooring or damp carpet at one spot
  • Low hot water pressure with no appliance problem
  • A sudden rise in the water bill

One sign is a clue. Several together are a strong reason to schedule detection quickly, since a slow leak under concrete slowly erodes the soil that supports the slab.

Why Slab Leaks Happen Around the Bay

Most Florida homes sit on a concrete slab, and supply pipes often run through or beneath it. Copper lines can corrode from the outside in aggressive soil, and a pinhole forms. Pipes can also rub against rock or concrete as the slab shifts, and that wear opens a leak over time.

Sandy coastal soil and a high water table after heavy rain keep the ground moving. Homes in older parts of Panama City often have copper from the 1970s and 1980s, which is the age range where pinhole leaks become common. Newer homes with PEX can leak at fittings.

How We Locate the Leak

The process starts with isolating the system. We test the hot and cold sides separately and shut off branches until the pressure holds. That tells us which line is leaking before we choose a tool.

Then we use acoustic listening to hear the leak through the concrete, thermal imaging to read warm lines on a hot-water leak and, where appropriate, tracer gas. Combined, they usually narrow the leak to a small spot on the floor. More on those tools is on our leak detection page.

Repair Options and Honest Trade-Offs

Slab leak repair options compared
OptionHow it worksTrade-off
Spot repair through the slabCut and open the concrete at the leak and replace the sectionFloor covering and concrete are disturbed at that spot
Reroute overheadAbandon the leaking line and run new pipe through walls and ceilingsMore work in the house, no concrete cutting
Epoxy pipe liningCoat the inside of the pipe to seal the leakSuits some conditions and not all, needs the right pipe layout
Partial repipeReplace the failing run or the full systemHigher cost, fewer repeat repairs, see repiping

There is no single correct answer. A single pinhole in an otherwise healthy system favors a spot repair. Several leaks over time point toward rerouting or repiping. We lay out each path with what is disturbed and what it costs.

Floors, Insurance and Permits

Flooring decides how disruptive a spot repair feels. Tile can be lifted and relaid, hardwood and luxury vinyl are harder. We protect the surrounding area and tell you in advance what will be removed. Slab work may require a permit, and we ask the local building department what applies.

Insurance policies treat slab leaks differently. Some cover the resulting water damage and some also cover access costs. Keep photos, the leak report and invoices together for your adjuster.
