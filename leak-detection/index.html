Suspect an Active Leak? Do This Right Now

Call (855) 575-2890 to talk through the problem and ask about timing. Shut off the main water valve if water is visible or a ceiling is sagging. Then turn off the breaker for any room with wet walls or ceilings, and move belongings out of the wet area.

If you are not sure whether there is a leak, do a quick meter check. Turn off every tap and appliance, then look at the meter dial or leak indicator. If it moves, water is leaving the system somewhere, and you have something to report.

How Leaks Get Found

Finding a leak is a process of elimination. We start with the meter and a pressure test to confirm there is a leak and whether it sits on the supply side before the house or inside it. Then we narrow the zone with listening equipment, tracer gas or thermal imaging.

High Water Bill With No Visible Leak

A bill that jumps without a change in habits is a common trigger for this call. A running toilet flapper, a leaking irrigation valve or a slow supply line leak can add thousands of gallons a month without any water on the floor. The meter test separates a household leak from heavy use.

In beach rentals the pattern is familiar. A house that sat quiet for a week can show a dripping outdoor spigot or a stuck toilet fill valve that no one noticed. If you manage a rental near Laguna Beach, checking the meter between guests is cheap insurance.

Walls, Ceilings and Behind-the-Wall Leaks

A stain on drywall is where the water ended up, not necessarily where it started. Water travels along pipes and framing before it shows. Thermal imaging and a moisture meter trace the damp area back toward the source.

When the leak is in a supply line inside a wall, we open the smallest possible access. If the leak is under a concrete floor, the cause and the options are covered in our slab leak repair guide.

Crawlspaces, Irrigation and Pool Lines

Older Panama City homes often sit on raised foundations with crawlspaces, where a leak can go unseen for months and feed mold and rot. We inspect with a light, a moisture meter and, where needed, listening equipment. Crawlspace work is cramped and we describe what we find before any repair.

Lawn irrigation lines and pool supply lines are common leak sources in Florida yards. A buried irrigation line may leak into the soil with only a soft spot to show for it. We test the zone, locate the break and repair the line or tell you if it belongs to a landscape specialist.

After the Leak Is Found

Finding the leak is only half the job. The repair depends on what and where the pipe is, from a simple fitting swap to a burst pipe repair or a section replacement. If the pipe is failing along its length, we discuss repiping rather than patch after patch.

We also document what we found. Photos and meter readings help with insurance claims and, for rentals, with notice to a property owner.
