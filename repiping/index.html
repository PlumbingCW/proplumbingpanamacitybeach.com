Whole House Repiping in Panama City Beach, FL

When pinhole leaks keep returning, water runs brown or pressure drops through the house, patching stops making sense. A repipe replaces the supply lines so the system is sound again.

Signs Your Pipes Are Due for Replacement

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different spots
  • Brown or rusty water, especially after sitting
  • Low pressure that gets worse over time
  • Visible green corrosion on copper or flaking on galvanized lines
  • A past recall or known problem pipe in your house

One leak is not a reason to repipe. A pattern of leaks usually is. Our burst pipe and leak detection teams can tell you whether you have a single failure or a failing system.

Pipe Materials in Local Homes

Homes built before the 1980s may still have galvanized steel, which rusts from the inside and restricts flow. Copper from the 1970s through the 1990s is common and can pinhole in aggressive water or soil. Polybutylene, used in some homes through the mid-1990s, is a known failure risk and a common reason for repiping.

In older parts of Panama City, you will see a mix of materials from different renovation periods. Homes repaired after storms may combine old and new pipe. We map what you have before recommending anything.

PEX or Copper?

Repipe materials compared
MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, quicker installationMust be protected from sunlight and some chemicals
CopperLong track record, tolerates heat and sunlightHigher material cost, can corrode in aggressive conditions
CPVCLower cost, rigidCan become brittle with age and heat

PEX is the most common choice for whole-house repiping because it goes in with less demolition. Copper is chosen for exposed runs or by owner preference. We explain both and use what suits your house.

What the Work Looks Like

  1. Inspect the house, locate shutoffs and plan the new routes
  2. Pull permits where required
  3. Install new lines through attics, walls or crawlspace, in phases if you want
  4. Connect fixtures and test the system under pressure
  5. Patch the small access openings or coordinate with a drywall contractor

Slab homes often reroute overhead instead of cutting concrete. That approach is also an option after a slab leak when the system as a whole is failing.

Living in the House During a Repipe

Water is typically off for part of the day, and we plan around it. For rentals, scheduling between guests matters. Vacant homes and seasonal properties are easiest, because there is no one to disturb and the work can move quickly.

Dust and drywall openings are part of the job. We protect floors, cut small access holes and tell you ahead of time which rooms will be affected.

Insurance, Resale and Timing

Some insurers ask about pipe materials, and certain older types can affect coverage or premiums. If you are buying a home with polybutylene or galvanized pipe, ask your insurer what they require. A repipe done before listing can also remove a concern for buyers, though it will not always return its cost at sale.

Timing matters, too. A planned repipe lets you pick the materials, schedule around occupancy and choose the season. An emergency repipe after a flood leaves little room to choose. If your system is showing the warning signs, plan the work before the next leak forces your hand.
