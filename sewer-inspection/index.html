What a Sewer Camera Inspection Shows

The camera head carries lights and a lens and pushes through the line under its own cable. A screen at the cleanout shows the inside of the pipe in real time. Cracks, offset joints, roots, scale, sagging sections and collapses are all visible.

A locator can find the camera head from the surface, so we can mark where a problem sits and how deep it is. That turns a vague suspicion into a specific spot on your property, which is what a repair estimate needs.

When an Inspection Pays Off

Most calls fall into a few categories. The first is a line that keeps backing up after cleaning, where the camera finds the cause. The second is a home purchase, where a seller’s plumbing history may be thin and a sewer line is expensive to discover wrong after closing.

The third is storm aftermath. Hurricane season runs June through November on the Gulf coast, and flooding or shifting soil can damage buried lines without any visible sign. A scope after a major storm shows whether the lateral came through intact.

Buying or Selling in Panama City Beach

Many beach properties change hands as second homes or investment rentals, and buyers often rely on a general home inspection that does not include the sewer lateral. A camera pass gives a buyer a recorded view of the line from the cleanout to the main connection, and gives a seller documentation if the line is sound.

If the footage shows a defect, you can decide whether to negotiate, request a repair or walk away. Repair paths such as lining or replacement are covered on our sewer line repair page, and clearing a blockage that the camera uncovers is handled under sewer line cleaning.

What You Receive After the Scope

  • A recorded video of the run that you can keep
  • The location and approximate depth of any defect, marked on the surface
  • A plain explanation of what each defect means
  • Repair options with the trade-offs, if repair is needed
  • A statement when the line looks sound, so you can move on

The report does not oversell. A line with a minor offset that has never caused a backup may only need monitoring, and we will tell you so.

Limits of a Camera Inspection

A camera needs a path. A cleanout, an accessible vent or a pulled toilet gives access. Heavy blockages must be cleared first, and a collapsed section can stop the camera from going further. We note where the run ended and why.

A camera also shows condition today. It cannot predict when an aging pipe will fail. If the footage shows scale or thin cast iron, we discuss what that usually means for the next few years and what to watch for.
