How to Choose a Plumber

  • Ask for the license number and check it on the Florida DBPR license lookup.
  • Ask whether the plumber carries liability insurance and request proof if the job is large.
  • Ask who pulls the permit and what inspection follows.
  • Ask what warranty covers the parts and the labor, and for how long.

These questions work for any plumber and any job. A clear answer to each is a good sign, and a vague answer is a reason to keep looking.

After the work, ask for an itemized invoice that lists parts, labor and any permit, and keep it with your home records. A written summary of what was found and what was done helps with warranty claims, insurance reports and a future sale. If a landlord or condo association is involved, send them a copy as well.

Licensing and Permits

Plumbing in Florida is a licensed trade, and contractor licensing is handled by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation. Before you hire any plumber, ask for the license number and check it on the state license lookup. Plumbing work here follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).

Permit rules for plumbing work vary by jurisdiction, so ask the local building department what applies to your address. Ask whoever does the work who pulls the permit.

What Affects the Price of a Plumbing Job

No honest figure can be given for a job nobody has seen. Several things move the price of almost every plumbing job, and knowing them helps you compare quotes fairly.

AccessA pipe behind an open cabinet is quicker to reach than one inside a wall or under a slab.
MaterialsPipe type, fixture grade and the brand of a water heater all change the parts cost.
ScopeA spot repair and a full replacement are different jobs, so compare quotes line by line.
Permits and codeSome work needs a permit and an inspection, which adds steps to the schedule.

What to Have Ready When You Call

A short, clear description saves time on any plumbing call. Know the address, which fixtures are affected, when the problem started and whether the main water valve is shut. If a water heater is involved, read the brand and model from its label.

If water is actively flowing, shut off the main valve first and move valuables out of the way. Photos of the damage and the equipment help with insurance as well as with diagnosis.

Plumbing in a Beach Community

Panama City Beach has salt air, sand, seasonal occupancy and many condos, and each affects plumbing. Salt air corrodes exposed fittings and valves. Sand collects in shower drains and traps. Homes that sit empty between guests can dry out trap seals and then take a heavy load in one weekend.

Condo buildings add shared risers and stacks, where a problem in one unit can show up in another. Associations often set rules for shutoffs, work hours and contractor paperwork, so ask the manager before work begins.
