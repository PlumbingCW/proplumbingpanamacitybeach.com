Disposal Stuck, Humming or Leaking? Safe Steps

Turn off the disposal switch and flip the breaker. Never put your hand into the disposal, even when it is off. If water is leaking under the sink, close the angle stop valves and place a bucket underneath. Call (855) 575-2890 to talk through the problem and ask about timing.

Do not run water into a jammed disposal. It will back into the sink and fill the cabinet if there is a leak. Keep the area clear and take a photo of the model label if you can, since it helps us bring the right parts.

Why Disposals Fail

The usual causes are jams from bones, fruit pits or fibrous food, worn seals that leak at the top or the bottom, and grease that coats the grinding chamber and the drain line. A humming noise with no spin means the motor is working against a jam. Silence means a tripped overload or a dead motor.

Vacation rentals put disposals under heavy and unpredictable use. Guests drop shells, rice and fats into them, and the grease builds into the drain beyond the disposal. A clog downstream looks like a disposal failure, so we check the trap and the branch line before replacing the unit.

What We Check and Fix

  1. Test the switch, outlet and reset button to rule out power problems
  2. Free a jammed unit using the hex socket at the bottom or a wrench
  3. Inspect the mounting flange, gasket and drain connections for leaks
  4. Clear the sink trap and the branch line past the disposal
  5. Recommend repair or replacement based on age and condition

If the sink stays slow after the disposal is working, the blockage is further down. Our drain cleaning service handles that part of the line.

Repair or Replace?

A jam is a quick repair. A leak from the housing seam or a motor that no longer starts usually means replacement. Disposals have a service life, and a unit with several repairs behind it is a good candidate to replace.

When replacing, we match horsepower to your use and check the mounting, dishwasher drain connection and electrical supply. A kitchen with a dishwasher needs the knockout plug removed on a new disposal, and we handle that step.

Installing a New Disposal

A new installation involves the sink flange, the mounting assembly, an electrical connection and a dishwasher drain tie-in. Wiring a hardwired unit calls for a licensed professional in many cases. If you are weighing a do-it-yourself install against a licensed plumber, the state licensing board lists what requires a license and our plumbing installation page covers related fixtures.

Keeping a Disposal Healthy

  • Run cold water before, during and after use
  • Keep grease, coffee grounds and fibrous scraps out
  • Never put bones, pits or large shells in the unit
  • Run a little water through it between rental guests to keep seals wet
