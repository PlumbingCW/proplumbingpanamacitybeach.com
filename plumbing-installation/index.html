What We Install

  • Toilets, sinks, faucets, tubs and showers
  • Garbage disposals and dishwasher connections
  • Water heaters and tankless units
  • Outdoor spigots, hose bibs and shutoff valves
  • Supply and drain lines for remodels and additions
  • Water filters and softeners

Installation differs from repair because the layout is in your hands. We help you choose fixtures that fit your drain, supply and space before you buy, and we install what you chose or recommend alternatives.

Remodels and Additions

A kitchen or bath remodel changes where water and waste move. Moving a sink or adding a shower means checking drain slope, vent paths and supply capacity. On slab homes, new drain lines may require cutting the slab, and we plan that before demolition starts.

Adding a bathroom to a beach house also raises capacity questions. A larger water heater, a bigger main or an upgraded line may be needed. We check before the drywall goes up.

Rental and Vacation Property Upgrades

Owners often upgrade fixtures between seasons. Durable faucets, water-saving toilets and shutoff valves at each fixture reduce repair calls. Leak sensors and accessible shutoffs make it easier for a manager to catch a problem before guests arrive.

We install what makes a rental easier to maintain and tell you when a cheaper fixture will cost more in repairs. Related work is on our toilet repair and faucet repair pages.

Code and Permits

Florida plumbing work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, and local jurisdictions enforce it. Permit rules vary, so we ask the Panama City Beach building department what applies to your address. A permitted, inspected job protects you at resale and when you file an insurance claim.

Check any contractor on the state license lookup before you hire. A licensed plumber carries the credentials and insurance that unlicensed work does not.

Choosing Fixtures for a Salt-Air Climate

Fixtures near the Gulf face salt in the air and humidity most of the year. Finishes that look good in a showroom can pit and flake within a couple of seasons. Solid brass bodies, ceramic cartridges and finishes rated for coastal exposure last longer, and they cost a little more up front.

Exterior pieces such as outdoor showers, hose bibs and pool-side faucets deserve extra care. Stainless or brass hardware and a quick rinse now and then keep them working. We explain the trade-offs when you choose fixtures, so you are buying for the place the fixture will live.

Working With Your General Contractor or Designer

On a remodel, plumbing happens in two stages. The rough-in comes before walls close, and the finish comes after tile and cabinets are set. Fixtures need to be on site and the rough-in measurements confirmed before the rough-in is done, since a toilet or a vanity that does not fit the rough-in causes delay.

Send us the fixture model numbers when you can. We check the specification sheets against the layout and tell you if a drain location or a supply height will cause a problem. Catching it on paper costs nothing.

Keeping Water Off the Floor After the Install

A new install is a good moment to add small protections. Individual shutoff valves at each fixture let you stop one leak without losing the whole house. Braided supply lines last longer than rigid plastic ones. A drip pan under a water heater or an automatic shutoff device can limit a failure. These add little to a job and can matter a great deal in a rental.
