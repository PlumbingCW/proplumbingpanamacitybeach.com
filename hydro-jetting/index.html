What Hydro Jetting Actually Does

A jetter feeds a flexible hose with a nozzle into the line through a cleanout. Water leaves the nozzle at high pressure through angled jets, which pull the hose forward and scour the pipe wall as it travels. A cable cuts a channel through a clog. A jet removes the coating that caused it.

That difference matters on lines that clog again within weeks. Grease and scale layer up on the inside of a pipe, and a cable leaves most of that layer behind. A jetting pass restores much more of the pipe diameter, which is why it is the usual step after repeat drain cleaning calls.

Where Jetting Fits in Panama City Beach

Kitchens that serve beach crowds put a lot of fat, oil and starch into their lines. Restaurants and bars around Pier Park and Front Beach Road run heavy dishwashing on summer weekends, and a grease trap that is overdue can push fat into the building drain. Jetting clears the line, and the trap still needs its own service schedule. See our commercial plumbing page for after-hours kitchen work.

Homes get it too. Long horizontal runs under slab-on-grade houses, common in coastal Florida, move waste slowly, so grease and scale settle in the flat stretches. Older cast iron in parts of Panama City can also carry heavy scale that a cable will not shift.

When We Camera the Line First

Water at high pressure is the wrong tool for a damaged pipe. Before jetting, we look at the line with a camera when the symptoms suggest a break, a collapse or a deep belly. A cracked clay pipe or a separated joint can be made worse by pressure, and the camera tells us which situation we are in.

  • Jetting suits grease, scale, sludge and root hair on sound pipe
  • A camera first is wise on older clay, cast iron or Orangeburg lines
  • A collapsed section needs a sewer line repair, not more cleaning
