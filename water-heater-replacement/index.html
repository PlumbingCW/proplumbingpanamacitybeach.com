Tank Failed and Flooding? Shut It Down First

Call (855) 575-2890 to talk through the problem and ask about timing. Turn off the cold water supply valve on top of the heater, switch off the breaker or the gas control, and open a hot tap in the house to relieve pressure. Place towels or a wet vacuum on the floor and keep the area clear.

If the heater sits in an attic or above living space, shut the water off at the main valve instead. A failed tank can release dozens of gallons quickly, and ceiling damage follows fast. Photos of the damage help if you plan an insurance claim.

Signs a Water Heater Is Past Repair

  • Water pooling under the tank, not at a pipe fitting
  • Rust-colored hot water or flakes in the tap
  • Rumbling and popping that flushing does not cure
  • A tank past ten years with repeated repairs
  • Rusted jacket, fittings or a corroded base near a coastal garage

Any one of these on an older unit usually means plan for a replacement. If the unit is younger and the problem is a single part, water heater repair is the better first step.

Replacement Timing and What Delays It

Replacement timing depends on stock and on whether the new unit fits the old connections. Changes of fuel, relocating the heater, a new gas line or a venting change take more planning and may need a permit.

Permit requirements vary, so we ask the Panama City Beach building department what applies to your address. The permit steps are covered on our water heater installation page.

Condos, Rentals and Hurricane Season

We work with managers on those requirements.

During hurricane season, which runs June 1 through November 30, a heater in a garage that floods needs a full inspection before reuse. Floodwater can damage controls and burners even when the tank looks fine. Replace rather than reuse anything that has been under water.
