Infrared Leak Detection in Panama City Beach, FL

A thermal camera does not see water. It sees temperature differences that water leaves behind. Here is how infrared leak detection works, where it excels and where it needs backup.

What Infrared Imaging Actually Detects

An infrared camera reads surface temperature and shows it as a color map. Wet building materials usually sit at a different temperature than dry ones because water changes how heat moves and evaporates. A damp patch behind drywall can show up as a cooler or warmer area on the image.

The camera does not see through walls or detect water directly. It finds temperature patterns that suggest moisture or a hot line. A trained operator reads those patterns and confirms them with other tools.

Where Thermal Imaging Works Best

  • Hot water line leaks under floors, which warm the surface above
  • Radiant or hydronic heating lines
  • Wet drywall and ceiling areas after a supply or roof leak
  • Insulation soaked by a hidden leak
  • Finding the extent of damp areas before opening walls

On a hot line leak, the camera can show a warm trail on the floor that follows the pipe. That is a strong clue, though the exact break still needs confirmation.

Where It Struggles

Cold water leaks under thick concrete produce little temperature difference, and the camera may show nothing. Hot, sunny rooms can wash out the contrast. Foil-backed insulation or metal can mask or confuse readings. The camera works best in a stable room with the air conditioning conditions controlled.

For cold line and slab leaks, acoustic listening and pressure tests do more of the work. See our slab leak repair page for how those methods combine.

No single method is complete. We use infrared to survey and guide, then confirm with at least one other method before cutting anything.

What a Thermal Scan Visit Looks Like

  1. We review your symptoms and shut down water use if needed
  2. We scan walls, ceilings and floors with the camera
  3. We confirm suspect areas with a moisture meter and other tools
  4. We mark the likely leak location and explain the findings
  5. You get repair options before any opening is made

The scan can often be done without removing anything. If openings are needed, we make them small and aim them at the confirmed spot.

Thermal Imaging and Insurance Claims

Images are useful documentation. A report with thermal images, moisture readings and the repair finding supports an insurance claim. Adjusters like clear evidence of where the water came from and what it affected.

Keep copies of the images, the findings and your invoice. If the cause is a leaking supply line, a burst pipe repair may follow.

Preparing Your Home for a Thermal Scan

You can help the scan work better. Run the air conditioning at a steady setting for a few hours before the visit. Move furniture away from walls and floors you want checked. Tell the technician about recent water events, such as a past leak or a spill, since old dampness can show up on the image.

If you suspect a hot line leak, avoid running hot water just before the scan unless the technician asks you to. A steady baseline makes the temperature pattern easier to read.

Reading the Results Without Overreacting

A cool patch on a thermal image is a clue and not a verdict. It might be a leak, an uninsulated stud bay, a duct nearby or condensation. The technician cross-checks with a moisture meter and with what you have noticed in the room.

If the image and the meter agree, the case is strong, and the next step is to confirm the source line with a pressure test. If they disagree, the search continues with other tools.

Limits You Should Know About

Infrared is a screening tool. It is excellent at showing where to look and weak at proving a cause. A thermal scan can miss a slow leak that has not yet changed temperatures, and it can flag areas that have nothing to do with plumbing. Anyone who promises that a camera alone will find any leak is overselling.

That is why a good visit pairs the scan with other checks. The meter test confirms there is a leak. The pressure test tells you which line. The moisture meter confirms damp material. The thermal image ties it together and shows how far the dampness has spread.

Use the scan to plan repairs and to document damage. Do not use it as the only reason to cut into a wall.

Panama City Beach Note

High humidity in Panama City Beach can fool simple checks, because damp air and condensation cool surfaces just as a leak does. Air-conditioned homes also have cold spots near ducts. A thermal scan is read against those local conditions, and we confirm with moisture readings. For rentals in Laguna Beach or older homes in Panama City, we scan when the house is stable and the air conditioning has been running.
