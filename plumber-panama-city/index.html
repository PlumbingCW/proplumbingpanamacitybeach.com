Older Housing, Older Pipes: What Panama City Homes Deal With

Panama City is the Bay County seat and the largest nearby market, and a good share of its housing is older than the beach condos to the west. Streets in neighborhoods such as St. Andrews and Millville include homes from the mid-twentieth century, with a mix of renovations layered on top over decades. Pipes of that age behave differently than anything built in the last thirty years.

Cast iron drain lines scale and crack, galvanized supply pipes rust from the inside and clay sewer laterals let roots in at the joints. When one of those fails, the home may have several others of the same age waiting. A camera pass on a drain or sewer problem often shows more than the symptom you called about. See sewer camera inspection for how that works.

What to Do While You Wait

1

Stop the water

Close the main valve if water is flowing. Most homes keep it near the meter or where the line enters the house.

2

Protect the floor

Lift rugs and cardboard off wet floor and move electronics away from water.

3

Keep the line clear

Stop flushing and running sinks if a drain is backing up, so more water does not reach the blockage.

4

Open the way

Clear a path to the water heater, cleanout or meter box so the plumber can get to work quickly.

Why Storm History Still Matters in Bay County

Hurricane Michael came ashore in the Panhandle in October 2018 and left lasting damage across Bay County. Many Panama City homes have been repaired or rebuilt since, and renovations sometimes join new pipe to old runs. Those transitions, and any section that sat wet or exposed after the storm, are common places for leaks.

If you bought a repaired home and are not sure what is behind the walls, a leak check or a camera scope is a modest way to learn. Our leak detection page explains what is checked and how.

Commercial Corridors and Downtown Buildings

Panama City has older commercial buildings downtown and busy corridors with restaurants and retail. Those properties run grease lines, floor drains and large water heaters, and they often have shared walls and shared lines. We coordinate with managers, check who owns which line and document each call so costs land with the right party.

Neighborhood Details That Shape the Work

Crossing the Hathaway Bridge from the beach, the roads shift from resort corridor to city grid. Downtown, the older residential streets and the commercial corridors each bring different building ages and different plumbing. Restaurants and shops downtown run commercial drains, while many homes near the bay sit on slab foundations in sandy soil.

Lots near the bay can sit close to the water table after heavy rain, which makes slab leaks harder to ignore. If you hear running water with everything off, see slab leak repair before the damage spreads.
