Faucet Will Not Shut Off? Quick Steps

Look under the sink for the two small angle stop valves, hot and cold, and turn them clockwise to close. If they are stuck or do not stop the water, shut off the main valve and call (855) 575-2890.

Do not force a corroded valve. Salt air and age make stops brittle, and a snapped stem can turn a drip into a flood. Place a bucket under any leak and move cleaning supplies and stored items out of the cabinet.

Why Faucets Leak

Most drips come from a worn cartridge, washer or seat inside the faucet. Mineral buildup and age cause these parts to fail, and a drip that seems small can waste a surprising amount of water over a month. Leaks at the base of the handle usually come from worn seals or loose packing nuts.

Leaks under the sink come from supply lines, drain connections or the faucet body. Braided supply lines near the Gulf and the bay can corrode at the fittings. We check each connection before blaming the faucet.

Repair Versus Replace

  • Repair when the faucet is relatively new and only a cartridge or washer has failed
  • Replace when the finish is pitted, the body is corroded or parts are no longer available
  • Replace if you want a different style, a higher flow rate or a pull-down spray
  • Bring your own fixture if you have chosen one, and we install it

Shower valves are a related repair. A tub spout that drips or a shower that will not turn off often needs a new cartridge, and sometimes a new valve body. See leak detection if you suspect water behind the wall.

Sink Drains, Traps and Supply Lines

Fixture work often reveals other issues. A corroded trap, a loose slip nut or a failing supply line is cheaper to replace while the cabinet is open. We replace brittle lines and old stops during the visit when they are part of the problem.

Slow kitchen sinks often trace back to the drain rather than the faucet. If the sink drains slowly, our drain cleaning service clears the branch. A disposal under the sink is a separate repair covered on our garbage disposal repair page.

Rentals, Condos and Seasonal Homes

A faucet that drips unnoticed through a quiet week can raise a water bill and stain a cabinet. For rental homes, a drip check between guests catches most problems. Condo owners should know where the unit shutoffs are, since a failed supply line in a unit can reach the floor below.

Outdoor Spigots and Hose Bibs

Outdoor faucets live in sun, salt air and the occasional cold snap, and they fail more often than indoor fixtures. A dripping hose bib, a handle that spins without turning the valve or a leak behind the spigot inside the wall are the usual complaints. Replacing the valve is usually quick, and a frost-resistant model suits spots that see the odd hard freeze.

Add a shutoff inside the house for any outdoor line that has none, so you can isolate it without turning off the whole house. It is a small addition that makes the next repair much easier.
