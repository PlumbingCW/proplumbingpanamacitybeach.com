Start With a Test, Not a System

Water quality varies by address and by source. Before buying equipment, test your tap. A simple home kit or a lab test shows hardness and certain minerals, and your water utility can provide its annual quality report for the supply that reaches your meter. Treat the test as the starting point.

We do not guess at your water. We look at your results, the symptoms you see and your plumbing, then recommend a system that matches. If your water already tests fine, we say so and save you the cost.

Softener or Filter: What Each One Does

Water treatment options compared
SystemWhat it addressesWhat it does not
Water softenerHardness minerals that cause scale and spottingTaste, odor and many contaminants
Whole house carbon filterChlorine taste and odorHardness
Sediment filterSand and particlesDissolved minerals and taste
Under-sink filterDrinking and cooking water at one tapWhole-house scale

Many homes need only one of these, and a few need a combination. Equipment that does not match your actual problem costs money and maintenance with no benefit.

Why It Matters for Your Plumbing

Scale shortens the life of water heaters and tankless heat exchangers and clogs faucet aerators. If you have a tankless unit, scale is a constant maintenance item. See our tankless water heater page for how scale affects service.

Coastal homes also face salt air outdoors, but that is separate from your tap water. Salt on the exterior of fixtures and fittings is a different problem than hard water inside the pipes, and a softener will not change it.

What a Good Installation Includes

  • A bypass valve so you can isolate the system for service
  • Proper drain and overflow connections for a softener
  • Correct pressure and flow sizing for your household
  • Clearance for salt or filter changes
  • A walkthrough of maintenance and settings

Poor installation causes leaks, drain problems and low pressure. We handle the plumbing connections, and the system manufacturer provides the settings guidance. For related fixtures, see plumbing installation.

Rentals and Maintenance

A rental needs low-maintenance equipment. Salt refills, filter changes and resin care must fit your management routine.

Reading a Water Quality Report

Your water utility publishes an annual quality report, often called a consumer confidence report. It lists what was measured in the supply and how it compares with the standards. It reports the water in the system and not the water at your tap, so pipes inside the house and old fixtures can change what comes out of the faucet.

That is why a test at your own tap adds information. If the two disagree, the difference points to something inside your plumbing. We can help you read both and decide whether equipment is the answer or a pipe repair is.

Matching Equipment to the Problem You Have

Cloudy glasses and scale on a showerhead are not the same problem as a chlorine taste or a sulfur smell. Treat the one you have. Hard water spots call for a softener. A chlorine taste calls for carbon. A rotten egg smell often starts at the water heater and may be solved with a rod change or a flush, not with a filter.

Ask any seller what the system does, what it cannot do and what it needs each month. A clear answer is a good sign, and vague promises are a reason to look elsewhere. Our water heater repair team can check whether the smell starts in the tank.
