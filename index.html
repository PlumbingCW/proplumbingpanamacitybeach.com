What to do first

  1. Shut off the main water valve if water is flowing, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house.
  2. Turn off the heater, and switch off breakers for wet areas if you can do so safely.
  3. Stop using drains if sewage is backing up, and keep people and pets out of the affected room.

Plumbing That Fits a Beach Town

Panama City Beach plumbing works under heavy seasonal use. Homes and rentals along Front Beach Road and near Pier Park fill up in summer and sit quiet in winter. Pipes and fixtures that rested for weeks show weak points when a full house arrives, and a quick meter check between guests catches many leaks early.

Salt air is the other local factor. Properties near the Gulf of Mexico and St. Andrew Bay see corrosion on exterior spigots, water heater jackets, valves and fittings faster than inland homes. Along Thomas Drive and toward St. Andrews State Park, where bay and gulf are both close, checking exposed fittings each spring is worth the few minutes.

Many homes here sit on concrete slabs in sandy soil, so a hidden supply leak can run under the floor for a long time. Hutchison Boulevard and the 32407 area mix homes and commercial buildings, and across the Hathaway Bridge the older streets of Panama City bring cast iron and clay lines with their own failure patterns.

All plumbing work follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing, which is based on the International Plumbing Code (IPC).
